About

I am a PhD student in civil engineering at Arizona State University and a Graduate Research Associate in the SAVE Lab, where I work on construction worker safety: underground pipeline incidents, heat stress, mental health, and workforce development.

Before graduate school I was a project engineer on a 108 km highway corridor in Bangladesh, and that is still the work I measure everything else against. My days were cost estimation, scheduling, and field inspection. On a $6M roadway expansion I built and tracked the CPM schedule in Primavera P6 and MS Project, produced cost estimates and quantity take-offs in Bluebeam, and ran budget tracking, life-cycle cost analysis, and earned value analysis, holding 92% schedule adherence. In the field I carried out construction inspection and QA/QC across the corridor; density testing, aggregate gradation, asphalt mix design verification, and concrete cylinder testing against AASHTO and RHD specifications; and I pioneered prefabricated vertical drain ground improvement in Bangladeshi highway construction, inspecting more than 15,000 installations and cutting the consolidation period by 40%. I created the RFIs, reviewed contractor submittals, kept the daily reports, documented pay items, and marked up the as-builts.

Earlier, as a site engineer and BIM modeler, I handled more than 50 RFIs along with the submittals, change orders, and closeout paperwork behind them, supervised pile casting and material quality control, tracked material quantities, and built the models and drawings the field actually worked from. I am looking for a summer internship in field engineering, project engineering, estimating, or scheduling with a general contractor or a DOT.

The research came out of that experience. On the projects I saw, the gap between what a job was estimated to cost and what it was awarded for was treated as ordinary, so at Wyoming I measured it across more than 1,500 highway projects in four states and built a multi-tier machine learning framework estimating teams can follow at the item level. My interest in both halves is the same: whether a crew or an estimator can actually act on what the analysis says.

A machine learning framework for item-level cost estimation

2024 – 2026
Problem
State DOTs routinely see large gaps between the engineer's estimate and the awarded bid. WYDOT had observed deviations up to 25%, but nobody had established whether the gap was random noise or concentrated in identifiable items — and uniform estimating models treat every bid item as if it behaved the same way.
Tools
Python (scikit-learn), R, IBM SPSS.
Outcome
Average deviations of 21.41% (WYDOT), 19.10% (MDT), 48.20% (CDOT) and 24.00% (UDOT), concentrated in a small set of items rather than spread evenly. Funded by a competitive UTC research grant through CTIPS with USDOT and WYDOT as partners; published in the International Journal of Construction Management.

Tracking material deliveries from site video

2021 – 2022
Problem
Material deliveries are logged by hand at the gate, so site inventory drifts away from the paperwork and discrepancies only surface at reconciliation.
Tools
YOLOv5 object detection, deep learning pipeline in Python.
Outcome
83% accuracy on vehicle detection and 88% on quantity calculation. Accuracy fell in poor light and with partially occluded loads, which is the honest limit on deploying this at a real gate. Runner-up in the Builtech Fest case study competition at KUET.

A framework for how construction teams and AI divide the work

2026
Problem
Construction management literature discusses AI adoption at length but offers little structure for deciding which decisions stay with people, which move to models, and which need both.
Tools
AI-assisted literature screening, with manual verification of every included source.
Outcome
Accepted to the International Conference on Industrialized Construction (IConIC 2026). The framework is conceptual and still needs testing against live project decisions.
